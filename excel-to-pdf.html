How to Convert Excel to PDF

Why turn a spreadsheet into a PDF?

A spreadsheet is made to be edited, but that is not always what you want. When you send an Excel file, the other person can change the numbers by mistake, and the layout can look different on their computer. A PDF is read-only and looks the same everywhere, so it is the safer choice for sharing, printing, and keeping records.

Common ways people use it

  • Invoices and reports: Send a final version that clients can read but not change.
  • Price lists and catalogs: Share a clean table of products and prices.
  • Schedules and timetables: Turn a class, work, or event schedule into a file anyone can open.
  • Marks and attendance sheets: Print or share results without the formulas behind them.
  • CSV exports: Many apps and websites export data as CSV. Turn it into a readable PDF in a few clicks.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Add your file. Click “Select Excel or CSV file,” or drag an XLSX, XLS, or CSV file into the dashed box.
  2. Check what was found. A line shows how many sheets and rows the tool read, so you know the right file loaded.
  3. Choose a sheet. If your workbook has several sheets, pick one, or choose “All sheets” to put them all in one PDF. Each sheet then starts on a new page with its name as a title.
  4. Choose the orientation. “Auto” picks Landscape for tables with many columns and Portrait for narrow ones. You can also set it yourself.
  5. Choose the page size. Pick A4 or Letter, depending on the paper you use.
  6. Click “Download PDF.” A spinner shows while the file is made. Your PDF downloads on its own, with the same name as your spreadsheet.

The first row of your sheet becomes the table header, shown in red. Empty rows are skipped, and each page gets a page number at the bottom. For the best result, make sure your first row contains column names, and delete columns you do not need before converting. If a table is very wide, the text is made smaller and the extra columns continue on the next pages, with the first column repeated so you can still follow each row.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this Excel to PDF converter really free?

Yes. It is completely free, with no signup, no watermark, and no limit on how many files you convert. You can use it as often as you need.

Is my spreadsheet uploaded to a server?

No. Your file is read and turned into a PDF inside your own browser, so it never leaves your computer or phone. That makes the tool a good choice for private data such as payroll, invoices, and customer lists.

Which file types can I convert?

You can use Excel files (.xlsx and .xls) and CSV files (.csv). Password-protected files cannot be read, so remove the password in Excel first and then try again.

Will my colors, charts, and formatting be kept?

No. The tool copies the values in your cells into a simple table with a red header row and light striped rows. Charts, images, cell colors, and merged cells are not carried over. Dates and numbers appear as they are shown in the spreadsheet, and formulas show their results.

What if my sheet has a lot of columns?

The text is made smaller when there are many columns. If the table is still too wide for the page, the extra columns continue on the following pages, and the first column is repeated so each row is easy to follow. Choosing Landscape usually gives the best fit, and deleting columns you do not need helps even more.

Why do some characters look wrong in my PDF?

The PDF uses a standard font that covers English and most European languages. Text in other scripts, such as Chinese, Japanese, Arabic, or Hindi, may not display correctly, and emoji are not supported. Very large sheets with many thousands of rows can also take a while on older devices.
