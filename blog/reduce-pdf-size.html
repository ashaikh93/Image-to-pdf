How to Reduce PDF File Size for Free (2025 Guide)

6 min read

You finish a report, scan a contract, or turn a stack of photos into a PDF. Then you try to send it, and the file is too big. Many email services limit attachments to about 20 to 25 MB. Job sites and school portals often set even smaller limits. And a huge file is slow to upload, slow to download, and a pain to share on a weak connection.

The good news is that you can reduce PDF file size without paying for anything. In this guide, you will learn three free ways to make a PDF smaller, plus a few simple tricks to keep your files light from the start.

Why Are Some PDFs So Big?

Most of the time, the answer is pictures. A PDF with only text is usually tiny. But a PDF with scanned pages or photos can be many megabytes, because each image is stored in high quality. To shrink a PDF file, you mostly need to make the images inside it smaller.

Method 1: Compress PDF on Mac (Preview)

Every Mac has a built-in way to make a PDF smaller. It uses a setting called a Quartz filter, and it takes less than a minute.

  1. Open your PDF in Preview.
  2. Click File, then Export. Do not choose Save.
  3. Click the Quartz Filter menu and choose Reduce File Size.
  4. Give the file a new name so you keep your original.
  5. Click Save and check the new file size in Finder.

Heads up: This filter can be strong. Pictures may look softer or blurry afterward, and the file does not always get much smaller. Always check the result before you send it.

Method 2: Compress PDF on Windows

Windows does not have a built-in PDF compressor. But there are two free or low-cost tricks that help.

Option A: Use Microsoft Word. If you have Word on your computer, it can shrink a PDF for you.

  1. Right-click your PDF, choose Open with, and pick Word. Click OK if Word says it will convert the file.
  2. Check that the pages look right. Complex layouts can shift a little during this step.
  3. Click File, then Save As, and choose PDF as the file type.
  4. Select Minimum size (publishing online).
  5. Click Save.

Option B: Make the pictures smaller first. If your PDF is made from photos, shrink the photos before you build the PDF. Open each picture in Paint, click Resize, lower the size to around 50 to 70 percent, and save it. Then turn the smaller pictures into a PDF. Photos taken on a modern phone are far larger than any screen or email needs.

Quick Tips to Keep PDFs Small

  • Resize photos before you add them to a PDF.
  • Use JPG instead of PNG for photos.
  • Scan at a lower setting, such as 150 to 200 DPI, if you only need to read the pages on a screen.
  • Split a long PDF into two smaller files if it is still too big.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the quality get worse if I reduce PDF file size?

Usually, yes, a little. Compression works by making the images inside the PDF smaller or less detailed. Text normally stays sharp. Pictures may look softer. That is why you should check the new file and keep your original.

How small can I make a PDF?

It depends on what is inside. A PDF full of high-quality photos can often shrink by a lot. A PDF that is mostly text is already small, so there is little to gain. There is no magic number, so try a method and compare the sizes.

Is it safe to compress a PDF online?

It depends on the website. Many upload your file to their servers. For private documents, use Preview on a Mac, Word on Windows, or a tool that works fully inside your browser. The Image to PDF tool on this site never uploads your files.

Why is my PDF so large in the first place?

Large images are the most common reason. Scans, phone photos, and screenshots are often saved at very high quality. Embedded fonts and extra pages can add size too, but images are almost always the biggest part.

Conclusion
