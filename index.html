How to Convert Images to PDF

Why turn images into a PDF?

Photos and screenshots are easy to take, but they are awkward to share. Ten separate pictures can arrive out of order, get lost in a chat, or be rejected by a website that wants a single document. A PDF solves this. It keeps your pages in a fixed order, looks the same on every phone and computer, and is accepted almost everywhere.

Common ways people use it

  • Scanned documents: Photograph each page of a contract, form, or letter, then combine the photos into one PDF.
  • Job applications: Put your resume, certificates, and ID copies into a single file that recruiters can open easily.
  • Receipts and invoices: Collect photos of your expenses into one PDF for a refund or reimbursement claim.
  • School work: Turn photos of handwritten homework or notes into one file for your teacher.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Add your images. Click “Select images,” or drag JPG, PNG, and WEBP files into the dashed box. You can add many at once, and you can add more later.
  2. Check the thumbnails. Each picture appears as a small preview with its page number.
  3. Set the page order. Drag a thumbnail to a new spot, or use the small arrows, which are handy on phones. Click the red × to remove a picture.
  4. Choose the orientation. Portrait suits tall pages such as documents. Landscape suits wide pictures.
  5. Choose the page size. A4 and Letter fit standard paper. “Fit to image” makes each page match the size of its picture.
  6. Choose a margin. Pick None for edge-to-edge images, or Small or Big for white space around each one.
  7. Click “Convert to PDF.” A spinner shows while the file is made, and then your PDF downloads on its own.

For the sharpest result, use clear, well-lit photos. Each image becomes one page and is scaled to fit without being stretched. Transparent areas in PNG or WEBP images turn white. The whole process takes less than a minute, and you do not need an account or any software.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this image to PDF converter really free?

Yes. It is completely free, with no signup, no watermark, and no limit on how many PDFs you create. You can use it as often as you like.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Your images are read and turned into a PDF inside your own browser, so they never leave your device. This makes the tool a good choice for private documents such as IDs, bank papers, and signed contracts.

Which image formats can I convert?

You can use JPG (JPEG), PNG, and WEBP images, and you can mix them in the same PDF. Other formats, such as HEIC from some iPhone settings, are not supported. If you have HEIC photos, switch your iPhone camera to “Most Compatible” in Settings, or convert them to JPG first.

How many images can I put in one PDF?

There is no fixed limit. The real limit is your device’s memory, because everything is processed on your own computer or phone. If you have hundreds of high-resolution photos and the page slows down, try making several smaller PDFs instead.

Will the image quality get worse?

The tool saves each image inside the PDF as a high-quality JPEG, so photos look nearly the same as the originals. PNG and WEBP images are converted in this way too, and any transparent areas become white. Your original files are never changed.

Does it work on iPhone and Android?

Yes. Open the page in Safari or Chrome, tap “Select images,” and choose photos from your library. Use the small arrow buttons to change the page order, since dragging can be hard on a touch screen. When the PDF is ready, you will find it in your Downloads folder or in the Files app.
