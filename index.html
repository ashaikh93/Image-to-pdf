How to Use This Image to PDF Converter

Turning pictures into a PDF takes less than a minute, and you do not need an account or any software. Here is the full walkthrough, followed by some real-world examples.

Step-by-step tutorial

  1. Add your images. Click “Select images,” or drag JPG, PNG, and WEBP files into the dashed box. On a phone, tap the button and choose photos from your library. You can add files more than once, so it is fine to add a few now and more later.
  2. Check the previews. Every image appears as a small thumbnail with a page number. This is your chance to spot a wrong or blurry picture before you convert.
  3. Put the pages in order. Drag a thumbnail to a new spot, or use the small ◀ and ▶ buttons under it, which work well on touch screens. Page 1 becomes the first page of your PDF.
  4. Remove what you do not need. Click the red × on a thumbnail to delete it from the list. Your original file is never touched.
  5. Choose the orientation. Portrait suits documents and receipts. Landscape suits wide photos and screenshots.
  6. Choose the page size. A4 and Letter match standard paper. “Fit to image” makes each page the exact size of its picture, so there are no white bars. With Fit to image, the page shape follows each picture, so the orientation setting is not used.
  7. Choose a margin. None fills the page edge to edge. Small leaves about 1 cm of white space, and Big about 2 cm.
  8. Click “Convert to PDF.” A spinner shows while your file is made, and then the PDF downloads on its own as images.pdf. Rename it to something clear, such as “resume-and-certificates.pdf.”

Each picture is scaled to fit its page without being stretched, and transparent areas in PNG or WEBP images turn white. For the best results, use clear photos taken in good light, and hold your camera straight above the page. HEIC photos are not supported. On an iPhone, go to Settings > Camera > Formats and choose Most Compatible, and new photos will be saved as JPG.

Real-world ways to use it

  • Submitting a job application: Many employers ask for one PDF with your resume, cover letter, and certificates. Add each page in the right order, choose A4 or Letter, and convert. One tidy file is much easier for a recruiter to open than five separate attachments.
  • Scanning receipts for taxes: Take a photo of each receipt as you get it. At the end of the month or year, combine them into one PDF in date order, so your records are in one place and easy to send to an accountant. Check your local tax rules to see what kind of digital records you are required to keep.
  • Combining photos into an album: Add pictures from a trip or an event, choose Landscape with Fit to image and a small margin, and you get a simple photo book that you can share as a single file.
  • School work and insurance claims: Bundle photos of handwritten homework, or pictures of damage for a claim, in the order they should be read.

Why Use This Tool? Privacy Matters

Your files never leave your device

Most online converters upload your pictures to a remote server, make the PDF there, and send it back. You usually cannot see where your files go or how long they are kept. That is a real concern when the pictures are IDs, bank statements, medical papers, or signed contracts.

This tool works differently. Your browser reads the images from your own device and builds the PDF right on the page, using an open-source library called jsPDF. Nothing is sent to our servers, so we never see your files, and we cannot lose or share them. You can check this yourself: open your browser’s developer tools, go to the Network tab, and convert some images. You will not see your pictures being sent anywhere.

Simple, fast, and free

  • No account and no installs. It works in any modern browser on Windows, Mac, Linux, iPhone, and Android.
  • No watermark and no daily limit. Your PDF is clean, and the only limit is your device’s memory.
  • Quick results. Because there is no upload or download of your files, the speed depends on your own device rather than your internet connection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this image to PDF converter really free?

Yes. It is completely free, with no signup, no watermark, and no limit on how many PDFs you create. You can use it as often as you like.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. Your images are read and turned into a PDF inside your own browser, so they never leave your device. This makes the tool a good choice for private documents such as IDs, bank papers, and signed contracts.

Which image formats can I convert?

You can use JPG (JPEG), PNG, and WEBP images, and you can mix them in the same PDF. HEIC photos are not supported, so switch your iPhone camera to “Most Compatible” or convert them to JPG first.

How many images can I put in one PDF, and will the quality drop?

There is no fixed limit on the number of images. The real limit is your device’s memory, so a very large batch of big photos can be slow, and you may want to make several smaller PDFs. Each image is saved in the PDF as a high-quality JPEG, so your pictures look nearly the same as the originals.

Does it work on iPhone and Android?

Yes. Open the page in Safari or Chrome, tap “Select images,” and choose photos from your library. Use the small arrow buttons to change the page order, since dragging can be hard on a touch screen. When the PDF is ready, you will find it in your Downloads folder or in the Files app.

What is the difference between A4, Letter, and Fit to image?

A4 and Letter are standard paper sizes, so they are best if you plan to print or submit the PDF as a document. “Fit to image” makes each page match the size of its picture, which is best for photos and screenshots. Margins add white space around each image on any of these.
